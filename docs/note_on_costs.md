While there is no single, physical "map" pinned to a wall that every reformer agrees on, yes, economists, free-market organizations, and healthcare disruptors have extensively mapped the regulatory "strangleholds" and "cartels" that artificially inflate U.S. healthcare costs.
In the current landscape (as of late 2025), this "map" is largely defined by the work of groups like the Mercatus Center, the Cato Institute, the Free Market Medical Association, and recent government efficiency initiatives (like the Department of Government Efficiency or DOGE concepts often discussed in this timeline).
Here is the "map" of the system’s bottlenecks and the specific low-hanging fruit identified by these experts to break the "cartels."
1. The "Cartel": Hospital Monopolies
The Mechanism: Large hospital systems buy up independent practices and smaller hospitals to kill competition. They then use their size to demand secret, higher prices from insurers.
 * The Regulation: Certificate of Need (CON) Laws. In many states, you literally cannot build a new hospital or buy an MRI machine without permission from a government board—a board often controlled by the existing hospitals. This is a classic "protectionist racket."
 * The Low-Hanging Fruit: Federal incentives for states to repeal CON laws.
   * Impact: Immediate increase in competition. Ambulatory Surgery Centers (ASCs) and independent imaging centers would pop up, offering cash prices often 5–10x lower than hospital rates for the exact same service.
2. The "Cartel": The AMA & Residency Bottlenecks
The Mechanism: The supply of doctors is artificially capped. The American Medical Association (AMA) and other bodies have historically lobbied to limit the number of government-funded residency slots, creating a doctor shortage that keeps salaries and prices high.
 * The Regulation: Scope of Practice Laws and Residency Caps.
 * The Low-Hanging Fruit:
   * Expand "Scope of Practice": Allow Nurse Practitioners (NPs) and Physician Assistants (PAs) to practice to the full extent of their training without needing a doctor to "sign off" (and take a cut of the fee).
   * Uncap Residencies: Change the funding model to allow more medical graduates to become practicing doctors, flooding the market with supply.
3. The "Cartel": Pharmacy Benefit Managers (PBMs)
The Mechanism: PBMs are the middlemen between drug manufacturers and insurers. They negotiate "rebates" (legal kickbacks) that incentivize high list prices. They profit when drug prices go up, not down.
 * The Regulation: "Safe Harbor" protections that allow these kickbacks to be legal under anti-kickback statutes.
 * The Low-Hanging Fruit: Eliminate Rebate Safe Harbors.
   * Impact: If PBMs are forced to pass discounts directly to patients rather than keeping them, the incentive to choose the most expensive drug disappears.
4. The "Cartel": The Insurance-Hospital "Billing Game"
The Mechanism: Hospitals charge "Facility Fees"—charging you a hospital room rate even if you just saw a doctor in a standard office building that the hospital happens to own.
 * The Regulation: Site-Based Payment Differentials (Medicare pays more for a service if it's "hospital-based" than if it's "independent").
 * The Low-Hanging Fruit: Site-Neutral Payments.
   * Impact: Mandating that Medicare (and forcing private insurers to follow suit) pay the same price for a service regardless of the building it happens in. This alone is estimated to save hundreds of billions over a decade.
5. The "Cartel": Opacity / Secret Pricing
The Mechanism: Prices are hidden until after care is received, preventing any market pressure.
 * The Low-Hanging Fruit: Enforcing "Real" Price Transparency.
   * While rules exist, hospitals often bury data in unreadable files. The "fruit" here is standardizing this data so apps (like Kayak or Expedia for healthcare) can easily compare prices for consumers.
Who has the "Map"?
If you are looking for the specific blueprints, look at the publications from these groups:
 * The Mercatus Center (George Mason University): They have a literal project called "The Healthcare Openness and Access Project" (HOAP) which maps state-by-state regulations that can be cut.
 * The Free Market Medical Association (FMMA): Founded by Dr. Keith Smith (Surgery Center of Oklahoma), they have mapped out how to bypass insurance entirely using cash pricing, exposing the artificial inflation of the status quo.
 * Paragon Health Institute: A newer group heavily focused on outlining these exact deregulatory steps for policymakers.
Summary Table of the "Low Hanging Fruit"
| Problem Area | The "Cartel" Mechanism | The "Low Hanging Fruit" Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Hospitals | CON Laws (illegal to compete) | Repeal CON Laws (allow independent centers) |
| Doctors | Licensing/Scope restrictions | National Reciprocity & Full Practice Authority for NPs/PAs |
| Billing | Site-Based Billing (upcharging) | Site-Neutral Payments (same price for same service) |
| Drugs | PBM Rebates (legal kickbacks) | Pass-through Rebates (money goes to patient) |
